# Entities

## Introduction

This layer holds the enterprise-wide business rules:

- These rules describe what the business does
- They remain relevant even if no software existed
- They are least likely to change when application changes

:::info
For example, a bank that charges a fixed interest rate on a loan applies that rule even if a clerk does the arithmetic by hand.
:::

An **entity** is an object that encapsulates critical business data with the core rules that operate on it.

Examples: Customer, Order, Product, Pricing rules


## Content

Can contain:
- Domain entities.
- Enterprise business rules.
- Business invariants.
- Domain behavior.
- Business policies.
- Relationships between core business concepts.
- Fundamental calculations and validations.


## Characteristics

- Their shape follows the business concept.
- Can be reused across multiple applications within the same business domain.
- Highly stable compared to other layers.
- Independent of any particular application, infrastructure and technical implementation.
- May be stored in a database, but should not know about the database.


## Dependencies

- Can depend on other entities.
- Should not depend on application-specific workflows, interface adapters, frameworks, databases, or external services.
